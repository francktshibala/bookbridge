# WHY NO ONE IS DATING ANYMORE — FULL SCRIPT

**Target Length:** 18-20 minutes (~2,800-3,200 words)
**Voice:** Male or female, calm, thoughtful, slightly emotional
**Tone:** Documentary-style, balanced, not preachy

---

## HOOK (0-10 seconds)

Half of young men in America are single right now. Not by choice. And here's the part that will shock you: most of them have given up completely. They're not even trying anymore. And no one is talking about why.

---

## PART 1: THE PROBLEM (10 sec - 3 min)

Something strange is happening.

Young people aren't dating anymore. They're not getting married. They're not having kids. And if you ask them why, most of them will tell you the same thing: "I've given up."

Let me show you what the data says.

A 2024 survey found that nearly half of young men aren't dating at all. Not casually. Not seriously. Not at all. And it's not because they don't want to. It's because they've stopped trying.

On the other side, young women are pulling back too. They're deleting their apps. They're choosing to stay single. Some are saying they'd rather be alone forever than deal with what dating has become.

Thirty percent of Americans between 18 and 34 now report feeling lonely every single day. Not sometimes. Every day.

And here's what's scary: loneliness isn't just a feeling. Researchers say it carries the same health risk as smoking fifteen cigarettes a day. It increases your risk of heart disease by 29 percent. Stroke by 32 percent. Dementia by 50 percent.

But it gets worse. The number of men who say they have no close friends has increased five times since 1990. Five times. In just thirty years, we've created a generation of men who have no one to talk to.

And when people have no one to talk to, they start listening to the wrong voices. They fall into online communities that tell them the world is against them. They radicalize. They give up. Some of them don't make it.

Men die by suicide at four times the rate of women. Four times. And loneliness is one of the biggest predictors.

This isn't just a dating problem anymore. This is a survival problem.

So what happened? How did we get here?

---

## PART 2: DATING APPS — THE PROMISE THAT FAILED (3-6 min)

Let's start with the thing everyone blames first: dating apps.

Tinder launched in 2012. Bumble came in 2014. Hinge in 2013. They promised to make dating easier. Swipe right, match, meet, fall in love. Simple.

Except it wasn't simple. It was a trap.

Here's something most people don't know: dating apps are not designed to help you find love. They're designed to keep you swiping.

Think about it. Hinge calls itself "the app designed to be deleted." But if everyone found love and deleted the app, Hinge would go out of business. Their success depends on your failure.

Match Group, the company that owns Tinder, Hinge, OkCupid, and forty-five other dating apps, has built a business model around keeping you single. The longer you stay on the app, the more ads you see, the more premium features you buy, the more money they make.

And it's working — for them. Match Group is worth over ten billion dollars. Bumble is worth over two billion. Meanwhile, only twelve percent of dating app users ever form a committed relationship.

Twelve percent. Out of fifty-three million users.

Seventy-eight percent of Gen Z now says they feel "burnt out" from dating apps. They're exhausted. They're frustrated. And they're quitting.

But here's the problem: what's the alternative?

Our grandparents met at church, at work, at community events. Those places still exist, but the culture around them has changed. People work from home now. Church attendance is down. Community groups are disappearing. The "third places" where people used to meet — coffee shops, bowling alleys, local clubs — are vanishing.

So young people are stuck. Dating apps don't work, but there's nowhere else to go.

And that's when things started to get ugly.

---

## PART 3: THE GENDER WAR (6-9 min)

When people are frustrated, they look for someone to blame. And in the dating world, men started blaming women. Women started blaming men. And the internet poured gasoline on the fire.

On one side, you have influencers like Andrew Tate telling young men that women are the enemy. That modern women are selfish, materialistic, and not worth the effort. That men should focus on money, status, and themselves. Millions of young men are watching this content every day.

On the other side, you have women saying they're done with men entirely. Last year, a question went viral on TikTok: "Would you rather be alone in the woods with a man or a bear?" Millions of women said they'd choose the bear. Not as a joke. Because they felt safer with a wild animal than with a strange man.

Think about what that means. We've reached a point where half the population doesn't trust the other half. Where men and women see each other as threats instead of partners.

And here's the tragedy: both sides have legitimate pain.

Women are exhausted. They've been harassed on apps. They've been on dates that felt unsafe. They've been ghosted, manipulated, and disrespected. They're not being dramatic. They're being careful.

Men are struggling too. Many of them feel invisible. They swipe right hundreds of times and get no matches. They're told they're not tall enough, not successful enough, not interesting enough. They feel rejected before they even get a chance.

A 2024 study found that young men are now more likely to report feeling "disconnected" and "irrelevant" than any other group. They don't feel like they belong anywhere. Not in relationships. Not in society. Not in their own lives.

Both sides are hurting. But instead of coming together, they're drifting further apart.

And there's a deeper reason why.

---

## PART 4: THE FATHERLESS GENERATION (9-12 min)

Here's something that doesn't get talked about enough.

Nearly one in four men in America grew up without a father in the home. One in four.

Think about what that means for a moment. These are boys who never had someone to show them how to treat a woman. How to handle rejection. How to be emotionally present. How to commit.

They didn't learn how to be a partner because they never saw one.

And even for men who did grow up with fathers, many of those fathers came from a different era. An era where men didn't talk about feelings. Where showing emotion was weakness. Where the father's job was to provide money, not connection.

So these young men enter the dating world with no map. No skills. No model for what a healthy relationship looks like.

And women can feel it.

Women today expect men to contribute emotionally, not just financially. They want partners who can communicate, who can be vulnerable, who can share the emotional weight of a relationship. But many young men have never been taught how to do any of that.

This isn't an excuse. It's an explanation.

When a young man has never seen a healthy relationship, he doesn't know how to build one. When no one ever taught him how to process rejection, he doesn't know how to handle it. When he's been told his whole life that emotions are weakness, he doesn't know how to open up.

And so he retreats. He plays video games. He watches content that tells him women are the problem. He gives up.

The boys are not okay. And we're not talking about it.

---

## PART 5: WHY WOMEN ARE CHOOSING TO STAY SINGLE (12-15 min)

Let me tell you about a woman I'll call Sarah. Her story is based on what thousands of women have reported.

Sarah is twenty-seven. She's smart, successful, and single. And she's choosing to stay that way.

Last year, Sarah went on twelve first dates. Twelve. Out of those twelve, three men made her feel unsafe. Two sent her unsolicited explicit photos after the date. One showed up drunk. And the rest? They just... disappeared. Ghosted. No explanation. Just gone.

Sarah isn't dramatic. She isn't picky. She's exhausted.

And Sarah isn't alone.

A 2024 study found that 57 percent of women on dating apps have received unsolicited sexual messages. Fifty-seven percent. More than half. And 37 percent have been harassed or threatened online.

But it's not just about the apps. It's about what happens when you actually meet someone.

Women today are more economically independent than ever. They don't need a partner to survive. They can buy their own house. They can support themselves. They can build their own lives.

So when a woman chooses to date, she's not looking for someone to take care of her. She's looking for someone who adds value to her life. Someone who makes her feel safe. Someone who respects her.

But here's what's happening: many women are finding that being single is easier than dealing with what dating has become.

They're tired of being ghosted. They're tired of being objectified. They're tired of being told they're "too much" or "not enough" or "too independent" or "too needy."

The bear question — "Would you rather be alone in the woods with a man or a bear?" — went viral because it wasn't a joke. It was a reflection of reality. Millions of women said they'd choose the bear because at least they know what to expect from a bear. A bear doesn't pretend to be something it's not. A bear doesn't gaslight you. A bear doesn't send you mixed signals.

Women aren't choosing to be alone because they hate men. They're choosing to be alone because they're protecting themselves. Because they're tired of being hurt. Because they've learned that being single is better than being in a relationship that makes them feel worse.

And honestly? Can you blame them?

---

## PART 6: WHAT'S ACTUALLY WORKING (15-17 min)

So where do we go from here?

Here's something interesting. Gen Z, the generation that's been hurt the most by all of this, is also the generation that's starting to change it.

They're quitting dating apps. A 2024 survey found that 79 percent of college students don't even use dating apps once a month anymore. They're done.

But they're not giving up on love. They're just looking for it differently.

And here's what's actually working: speed dating events are selling out. Friend groups are organizing mixers. People are going back to hobbies — not to find love, but to find community. And love is following.

In cities across America, there's a new trend: hobby-based meetups. Rock climbing gyms. Book clubs. Cooking classes. Board game nights. People are showing up not because they're desperate to find someone, but because they want to connect with other humans. And when you're not trying so hard, when you're just being yourself, that's when real connections happen.

Dating apps like Hinge are now investing millions of dollars to help Gen Z meet in real life because they know the apps alone aren't working. They're hosting events. They're partnering with local venues. They're trying to become what they were supposed to be all along: a way to meet people, not a way to keep you swiping.

Young people are also being more intentional. They're rejecting hookup culture. A recent survey found that Gen Z actually prefers monogamy over casual relationships — more than any other generation. They want something real. Something lasting.

There's also a growing movement of men who are rejecting the toxic content and looking for healthier models. Men who want to be better partners, better friends, better people. They're out there. They're just harder to find because they're not screaming on the internet.

The solution isn't better apps or more dating advice. The solution is simpler: put down your phone. Show up in person. Join a community. Be yourself. And let connection happen naturally.

---

## ENDING (17-20 min)

I want to end with this.

Half of young men are single right now. Not by choice. Millions of young women are single too. Also not by choice.

They're all waiting. For something to change. For someone to understand.

The young man swiping alone in his apartment at midnight — he's not the enemy. He's scared.

The young woman who chose the bear — she's not a man-hater. She's exhausted.

They both want the same thing. Connection. Understanding. Someone who sees them.

We've been told that men and women are at war. That we're too different to understand each other. That love is dead and dating is hopeless.

I don't believe that.

I believe we're going through a painful transition. The old rules don't work anymore, and we haven't figured out the new ones yet. We're lost. Both sides. Together.

But lost doesn't mean finished. Lost means we have a chance to find a new path.

Maybe the answer isn't better apps or more dating advice or another viral hot take. Maybe the answer is simpler than that.

Maybe we just need to start seeing each other as people again. Not profiles. Not statistics. Not the worst examples we saw online. People.

The person swiping next to you at midnight? They're not your enemy. They're just as lost as you are.

And maybe that's where we start. Not with better apps. Not with more advice. But with seeing each other as people again.

Share this if you know someone who needs to hear it. Because half of young men are single. Millions of women are single. And they're all waiting for something to change.

Maybe that starts with this video. Maybe it starts with a conversation. Maybe it starts with you.

---

**END**
